        How to use Sway and read your map

        Make one small choice. Quick pick records a preference as soon as you choose a card. Fine tune lets you move toward an item and then select Record preference. Clicking a card in Fine tune sets the strongest preview; it still needs submission. Equal records a tie, Skip adds no evidence, and Undo removes your latest recorded choice. Check Last recorded to see what was accepted.

        Read position and uncertainty together. Higher percentiles mean a higher expected position within your list. Five items in an exact order would have positions 0, 25, 50, 75, and 100; Sway averages over plausible orders. The dot shows expected position, and the band is an approximate 95% model interval. Wide bands leave room for a different order. They can stay wide around ties, even after many choices. A score is a relative position estimate, not a confidence percentage or a quality rating.

        Explore at your own pace. Coverage counts items that appeared in a recorded choice; it does not measure certainty. Uncompared items show a dash. Use Show all items to expand a compact map, and select See my order whenever the current perspective is useful. Keep refining resumes comparisons. There is no required finish point; a session can hold up to 10,000 recorded choices.

        Keep a recovery copy. Sway attempts to autosave one session in this browser. Save a JSON backup to restore your progress elsewhere; CSV exports are ranking summaries and cannot restore a session. If storage fails or another tab changes the saved session, download a backup before reloading or closing. Reloading adopts the saved version and discards this tab's unsaved work. Restore replaces the current session after confirmation. The app makes no automatic network requests; the research link below opens an external website.

        Use the keyboard. Tab to a comparison button to use left/right arrows for Quick pick, E for equal, S to skip, and Z to undo. On the Fine tune slider, use its native arrow keys to adjust your preview, then Enter to submit it. Comparison shortcuts apply only inside the comparison stage. Reduced-motion preferences are respected.

        About the estimates. Sway uses a Bayesian Bradley–Terry model for quick picks and a normalized continuous Bernoulli likelihood for strength judgments. It refits the full history after each choice, then uses a Gaussian approximation for utilities, analytic expected percentiles, and 1,000 joint samples for rank intervals. The model assumes stable, transitive preferences and conditionally independent judgments. Its response scale and intervals have not been calibrated with human participants. After connecting your list, pairing usually seeks information and explores less-compared options with probability 0.10. No comparison count guarantees a settled order.
